## 1. Characters
**Four styles as eras.** CDPR placed four styles on a timeline, and each one sets the look of fashion, architecture, vehicles and weapons:
- **Entropism** (the poor era): function over form, cheap materials, muted palette, harsh angles, little decoration.
- **Kitsch** (the recovery era): form over function, bright saturated plastic, rounded shapes, chrome and neon cyberware, colored hair.
- **Neomilitarism** (the corporate era): dark, sharp, utilitarian, power-projecting.
- **Neo-Kitsch** (the ultra-rich): colorful like Kitsch, plus rare "natural" signals such as animal prints and wood, because natural materials are scarce and expensive in the setting.

Artists said this gave everyone a shared vocabulary. Saying "build it in Entropism" was enough to tell the whole team how something should look. Weiss said she designs from sportswear, military wear and industrial design rather than from other cyberpunk art.

**Gang identities.** These notes are general lore from playing the game. They aren't quotes from the sources above.
- **Maelstrom:** heavy, visible chrome, face-replacing optics, black and red, an industrial horror look. The cyberware itself is the costume.
- **Tyger Claws:** Japanese street fashion with tiger and irezumi motifs, neon tattoos, katanas, bikes. Lots of decoration.
- **Valentinos:** gold, rosaries and religious iconography, tailored and flashy. Wealth plus faith.
- **Voodoo Boys:** pared-back, earthy, Haitian motifs, netrunner gear. Little visible chrome.
- **Animals:** huge, muscle-augmented bodies, sleeveless, primal. The body shape alone identifies them.
- **6th Street:** surplus military and patriotic gear (flags, camo). A Neomilitarism-flavored militia.
- **Mox:** punk Kitsch, pink and pastel, bold makeup and hair, an inclusive look.

The rule behind all of them: each gang is (one dominant style era) + (one signature motif) + (one signature body or cyberware trait) + (a 2–3 color palette). You can recognize a gang from 50 m by its body shape before you can make out its colors.

**Cyberware design language.** Cyberware runs from subtle seams (corpos) to fully exposed chrome (Maelstrom), and the amount of visible chrome signals social class and how far someone has gone. It is built from panel lines, seams and light strips, and arms and eyes are the main show pieces. Update 2.5 added **Glamdocs**, vendors who restyle arm cyberware (Mantis Blades, Gorilla Arms, Monowire, launcher) purely for looks. In other words, a functional part also became a cosmetic slot.

**Edgerunners adaptation.** Yoshinari (character design) tried to balance a Western game aesthetic with anime. He deliberately removed "cuteness" and leaned on silhouette, height and body-type differences. Imaishi said telling characters apart is hard in 2D Trigger style, so variety comes from shapes rather than detail. CDPR concept artists designed David's yellow jacket, while Trigger designed Lucy and Rebecca. The show uses flat color blocks, very saturated neon accents (the cyan/magenta/yellow "Trigger palette") and simplified cyberware readable as graphic shapes. One signature garment, David's jacket, carries the whole character and later becomes an in-game item. Update 2.5 repeats this with Weak Kingsley's full outfit and King's 5-piece set.

**Materials and texture (game).** Layered PBR: worn plastics, chrome, emissive strips, and decals and graffiti as a storytelling layer. The style era sets the material rules: Entropism is matte and cheap, Kitsch is glossy plastic, Neomilitarism is dark brushed metal and carbon, Neo-Kitsch is wood and fur.

## 2. Night City
- **Districts:** Watson (Kabuki, Little China, Northside industrial, Arasaka waterfront), Westbrook (Japantown neon, North Oak hills mansions, Charter Hill), City Center (Corpo Plaza towers), Heywood (Valentinos), Santo Domingo (industrial power plants, 6th Street), Pacifica (unfinished resort, Voodoo Boys), Dogtown (Phantom Liberty, a walled-off district inside Pacifica with Longshore Stacks and a stadium), and the Badlands around the city.
- **Distinct looks:** each district has its own story, a dominant style era and its own **color coding** (Knapik). Examples: neon-pink Japantown, sun-bleached abandoned Pacifica, cold blue-glass Corpo Plaza, rust and smokestacks in Santo Domingo, militarized ruins in Dogtown.
- **Time layers:** every street mixes eras, which lets players read a place's history without words.
- **Wayfinding:**
  - Tall structures act as a **compass**. The corporate towers in the center are visible from everywhere.
  - Kabuki is built on a **slope**: going downhill means going south.
  - **Iconic landmarks** like the huge "11" painted megabuilding.
- **Verticality:** Kabuki was built as "layers over layers" with **five stacked design paths**. Megabuildings are vertical neighborhoods with shops, residents and stories on each level. Longshore Stacks used height to place vendors, small stories and collectibles, with a central plaza tree as a gathering point.
- **Macro to micro planning:** an urban planner owned main roads, then local roads, pedestrian routes and the key story buildings. District leads built in parallel. **Districts were moved to tune travel time to player attention.** Some spaces were made uncomfortable on purpose to signal danger.
- **Non-linear levels (GDC talk):** every branch path must feel rewarding, not merely be available.
- **Density:** the dense core thins out toward the edges and the open Badlands. That contrast makes the core feel huge.

## Apply to Sky Rydah's
### Characters
1. **Write a 4-era style bible** for our city using our own names and lore, not CDPR's. For example: a scrap era (matte, angular, muted), a pop era (rounded, glossy, saturated), a corpo era (dark, sharp, chrome trim) and a nouveau-riche era (natural motifs). Tag every crew and district with a primary era so artists share one vocabulary.
2. **Crew formula:** era + one signature motif + one body or gear silhouette trait + a 2–3 color palette. Check that each crew reads in silhouette first, then in color. Draft assignments:
   - Code : E: corpo, sharp black with gold trim lines.
   - Static Angels: pop, rounded, halo or wing back piece, purple/cyan emissive.
   - Kogarasumaru: scrap street, ragged crow-feather collar or coat tails, black/red.
   - Anubis: nouveau-riche or ceremonial, tall jackal-ear headgear, teal with gold edges.
   - Behemoth: brute body type, huge shoulders, orange/black hazard stripes.
   - Sleeping Forest: natural motifs, long layered white cloak, deep green.
   - Genesis: pop with luxury, crown or sun-disc motif, magenta/gold.
   - Tool Toul To: scrap or industrial, tool rig and straps, rust/gray.
3. **Vary body type per crew** the way the Animals and Maelstrom differ: 2–3 proportion presets (lean, standard, heavy) on one skeleton so cosmetics still fit.
4. **Make the skates the "cyberware."** A visible-tech ladder (stock, then modded, then full chrome or emissive) works as both progression and status. Add a "Glamdoc"-style cosmetic restyle for skates and arm gear that doesn't change stats.
5. **One hero garment per crew** that carries its identity, like David's jacket. Put the crew emblem on the back for the chase cam.
6. **Trigger-style toon rendering:**
   - Flat 2–3 tone ramps and an inverted-hull outline on the silhouette only.
   - Saturated neon accents limited to emissive bands in our grey atlas.
   - No fine texture detail; show cyber seams as flat panel lines baked into the atlas bands.
7. **Map materials to era via the shading tier,** not via textures: per-band roughness/metal/emissive flags (scrap matte, pop gloss, corpo metal). It's the same single material, so draw calls stay low.
8. **Collab-style outfit sets:** full 4–5 piece crew or leader kits that unlock through story gigs, as with the King and Weak Kingsley sets.

### City/Map (7 districts)
1. **Give each district an era, a color key and a lighting mood.** Mixing in a second era as a "time layer" adds history. Use the color as a fog and sky tint plus emissive signage, which costs nothing on Quest.
2. **Use a central tower cluster as the compass,** visible from every district through a low-cost far-field impostor or skybox.
3. **Build in a slope gradient,** for example a hill on one side and low ground near the sea. Downhill means a known direction, and for skating it also means speed. Lay out downhill routes as fast lines.
4. **One giant landmark per district** that you can see from far away, like a painted megabuilding, a stadium or a giant sign. Use it as a "go to" waypoint and a grind magnet.
5. **3–5 stacked traversal layers per district** (street, rails and rooftops, cables and air), like Kabuki's five paths. For an Air Gear/JSR feel, rails and wall-rides should connect the layers.
6. **One megabuilding hub** that works as a vertical crew turf, with shops, NPCs, collectibles per floor and a central gathering-point landmark.
7. **Tune district spacing to time:** about 30–60 s of skating between district hubs, with shortcuts. Move districts around in blockout to fix pacing before art.
8. **Every branch rewards the player** with a collectible, a graffiti tag spot, a trick line or a view. Don't build dead-end optional paths.
9. **Plan the density gradient:** a dense core and open edges. On Quest, put dense geometry in the core behind occlusion (tall walls and canyons) and use open edges for sightlines with cheap impostors.
10. **Mark crew turf visually:** crew colors on graffiti, banners and light strips in the districts they control. Recoloring them when turf changes shows progress in the world.
11. **Make some spaces deliberately uncomfortable** (narrow, dark, harsh light) in rival turf to signal danger, and keep home turf open and bright.
